# MMORPG World Bible

## Purpose

This document defines the allowed worldview, narrative ceiling, and text style for the text MMORPG in this repository. It exists to keep new content coherent with the current starter-region scope already registered in code.

## World Positioning

- Genre: heroic fantasy with frontier exploration, light danger, and grounded daily life.
- Delivery form: text-first MMORPG content for chat interaction, not a dense novel or literary experiment.
- Narrative scale: local-region stakes first, wider world hints second.
- Mood baseline: hopeful, practical, mildly mysterious, occasionally tense.
- Violence level: moderate fantasy conflict. Avoid graphic gore and cruelty spectacle.
- Humor level: occasional dry humor and folk warmth are allowed; parody and internet slang dominance are not.

## World Core

- The current playable world is a frontier edge under the influence of the `dawn_alliance`.
- Civilization exists, but the region is still unstable: forests, mines, ruins, supply routes, and local monsters matter.
- The world should feel lived-in through labor, patrols, supply chains, inns, traders, healers, and minor rituals.
- Magic exists, but it should remain functional and legible. Avoid cosmology bloat unless the system can use it.
- Ancient ruins and lost histories are allowed, but they should reinforce exploration and danger, not overwrite the grounded frontier tone.

## Scope Limits

- Do not escalate every quest into a save-the-world crisis.
- Do not introduce continent-level wars unless the current region can actually reflect them.
- Do not add deities, demon lords, or cosmic powers as active plot drivers unless they remain distant and indirect.
- Do not turn every NPC into a hidden legend, exile prince, or apocalypse witness.
- Do not add highly technological or science-fantasy elements that clash with the current medieval-fantasy baseline.

## Faction Framing

- `dawn_alliance` should read as a practical coalition, not a perfect utopia.
- Factions should have needs, tradeoffs, and local interests.
- Faction conflict should mostly appear through patrol routes, logistics, taxes, trust, old grudges, and resource disputes.
- Villainy should usually be material and local before it becomes mythic.

## Region Design Philosophy

- Each area should have one main survival or travel pressure.
- Each area should have one memorable social function.
- Each area should have one believable loop tying NPCs, monsters, and resources together.
- Areas should connect through roads, trails, ruins, supply lines, rivers, work camps, or borders that make sense.

## Text Style

- Default language target: concise, clear, evocative Chinese when surfaced to players.
- Sentence rhythm: short-to-medium sentences, readable in chat, easy to skim.
- Description density: 1-3 strong sensory details, not full-paragraph purple prose.
- Preferred imagery: weather, stone, timber, iron, cloth, smoke, light, mud, tracks, bells, water, moss.
- Avoid repeated stock fantasy filler like “神秘而古老”, “充满未知”, “命运的齿轮”.

## Dialogue Style

- NPC dialogue should sound role-based, not writer-performed.
- Traders speak in price, risk, timing, demand, supply, and favors.
- Guards speak in routes, threats, orders, witness reports, and practical warnings.
- Healers speak in symptoms, rest, herbs, discipline, and limits.
- Trainers speak in mistakes, fundamentals, repetition, and craft pride.
- Quest givers should ask for concrete help, not theatrical exposition dumps.

## Tone Rules For Player-Facing Text

- Prefer “specific local stakes” over “vague epic prophecy”.
- Prefer “practical consequence” over “abstract destiny”.
- Prefer “credible frontier life” over “setting wiki exposition”.
- Prefer “one clear hook” over “five simultaneous mysteries”.

## Naming Rules

- Names should be pronounceable and stable in Chinese localization.
- Area names should imply terrain, history, labor, or atmosphere.
- NPC names should match role and region tone, not modern meme naming.
- Item, fish, and monster names should be descriptive first, exotic second.

## Forbidden Style Drift

- No modern social-media slang as the default narrative voice.
- No omniscient lore dumping in routine quest text.
- No explicit erotica.
- No shock-value torture writing.
- No random comedy skits that break setting tone.
- No abrupt switch into xianxia, cyberpunk, school life, or idol-industry storytelling.
